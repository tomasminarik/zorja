# Forecast API

Contract status: **v1 interface frozen, version 1.0.0**, served by the hosted service at `https://api.zorja.app` (POST only) and by the local server. The machine-readable authority is [api/openapi.json](../api/openapi.json). Fixtures in [api/examples](../api/examples) are reproducibly generated evaluator/serializer outputs from synthetic normalized inputs, not provider captures or live forecasts. The same contract describes actual local HTTP responses; scientific thresholds remain provisional.

**Current model:** modelVersion is `visibility-v0.2-bracket-prototype`, the retained-bracket model (called "Option A" on the [decisions page](decisions.md), 2026-10-13). It covers short-term activity experimentally, without a claim of scientific validation. Historical passages below describe the previous exact-instant model and do not override this. The immutable v1 freeze baseline is unchanged.

`now.activityEvidence` reports `retained-bracket-min-v1`, both forecastInstants/sourceIds and supportedInterval `[t1, t2)` when two already-received grids bracket now no more than 20 minutes apart. The local signal is their minimum bilinear overhead value. Source provider validity remains point coverage. An earlier source/weather/darkness boundary or five-minute recheck can shorten refreshAfter below t2. Clients withdraw current recommendations at refreshAfter and keep the separate 60-second request floor. No pair, excessive gaps, failed refreshes or stalled publication suppress current positive guidance. A three-hour local buffer persists across restarts; the hosted collector (every 10 minutes around darkness, every 30 otherwise) keeps it filled, but no cadence guarantees sufficiently close retained targets. Warm coverage depends on actual received inputs.

For OVATION, source freshness now uses a 20-minute publication/first-receipt deadline and a 20-minute deadline after the furthest retained target. Observation time remains provenance, not a ceiling that expires a forecast before its target. Identical-body/304 refetches do not reset first receipt. HTTP expiry controls refreshing; weather/Kp retain their prior cache/issue-age rules, except that since 2026-11-12 weather stays `fresh` for 3 minutes past its HTTP expiry (or past a successful check whose response was already expired), the time the next scheduled check can take; a failed check still goes stale at expiry. **Hosted service (since 2026-11-03):** the hosted service reuses a successfully checked OVATION grid for up to 15 minutes and Kp for up to 60 minutes; hosted Kp is `fresh` until 60 minutes after its last successful check (24-hour issue limit unchanged) and `refreshAfter` uses that boundary. Local behavior and the frozen contract are unchanged. The model version and policy identifiers explicitly identify this change; source statuses still describe the applicable freshness policy, not a visibility probability. Multiple `aurora_short_term` Source entries may exist; link by IDs and do not assume one source per role.

Optional nullable **`upcomingActivityInstant`** contains `{forecastAt, sourceId, overheadSignal, activity, temporalPolicy, spatialPolicy}`. It names the newest received future target; overheadSignal is a 0–100 provider-unit signal, not a chance of seeing aurora. Activity is `supported`, `unknown` (weak overhead signal), `not_covered` (missing interpolation cells), `stale` or `unavailable`; stale/unavailable signals are null. It is a model forecast at one instant, with no duration, weather/darkness rating or current action. It can exist when upcoming is null, including daylight/midnight sun. Null/absent means no newest future point, not zero activity. The backend alone applies the threshold; clients must not turn its activity into a viewing rating or alert. Clients may ignore this compatible optional addition or present it at its exact time.

## What Zorja forecasts — 2026-11-12

Three optional, additive fields implement [What Zorja forecasts](forecast-model.md#what-zorja-forecasts-2026-11-09). `apiVersion` stays 1.0.0, no existing field changes meaning, and the planning rating is unchanged (local planning activity stays unknown). Older clients may ignore all three; unknown enum values fall back to neutral presentation.

- **`auroraReach`** (Forecast, nullable): `{group, magneticLatitude, kpOverhead, kpLowInNorth, method}`. `group` is `under_zone` (absolute corrected magnetic latitude 62° or more), `edge` (55° to under 62°) or `south`. `magneticLatitude` is AACGM-v2 at 110 km from a precomputed 1° table (`backend/src/aurora_backend/astronomy/aacgm_table.py`, epoch 2026-11-01, regenerate with `backend/tools/generate_aacgm_table.py` when a new IGRF is released); bilinear lookup is within 0.006° of `aacgmv2`. `kpOverhead = (66 − m) / 2`, `kpLowInNorth = kpOverhead − 2`; either may be negative or above 9. Where AACGM is undefined the group is `south` with null values. `method` is `aacgm-v2-noaa-oval-v1`. Kp numbers are never shown in the app.
- **`planning[].auroraOutlook`**: `{status, highestKp, sourceId}`. `not_applicable` under the zone and at the edge. In the south, `could_reach` when the highest forecast Kp bin over the night's remaining dark windows (the remaining night period when it has none) is at least `kpLowInNorth`, else `not_expected`; `not_known_yet` when the fresh Kp forecast doesn't cover the whole span; `stale`/`unavailable` follow the Kp source. Places the rule never reaches (`kpLowInNorth` above 9 or null) are always `not_expected`. Elapsed dark hours are ignored, as in `globalKpContext`. `highestKp` is null unless `could_reach` or `not_expected`. It is a planning outlook: it never changes rating, action or alerts, `not_expected` doesn't mean impossible, and `could_reach` is not a promise.
- **`now.activeSince`** and **`now.activeSinceIsBufferLimited`**: only on `now`. When current activity is `supported`, the forecast instant of the first retained OVATION run in the unbroken active stretch that includes now (same overhead threshold, consecutive runs at most 20 minutes apart, within the last three hours); otherwise null and false. The flag is true when the stretch reaches the oldest retained run in that window, so it may have started earlier. It describes received model runs, not observed aurora, and is never carried forward.

Two synthetic examples cover the south: `south-could-reach.json` and `south-not-expected.json` (Edinburgh). The recorded replay fixture gained the same fields without other changes.

## Forecast endpoint

Hosted route policy (since 2026-11-02): the hosted service accepts forecasts by
POST only. Hosted GET returns 405 before evaluation, including trailing slash
forms. The repository contract, immutable baseline and local GET remain
unchanged. This guard cannot prevent ingress from logging a rejected query;
the hosted log check is in [privacy](privacy.md).

**Additive transport, 2026-11-01:** `POST /v1/forecast` accepts the same three inputs in a bounded JSON body and returns the same Forecast/Error schemas through the same evaluator and admission controls. GET remains unchanged. The body carries no query string, is limited to 1024 bytes and must arrive within three seconds. GET and POST share the request floor and retry rules. The app uses POST.

`GET /v1/forecast?latitude=59.3293&longitude=18.0686&timezone=Europe%2FStockholm`

Coordinates are WGS84 degrees. Require a valid IANA timezone to group local nights; the backend must not infer it from a UTC offset. Current proposal accepts all valid coordinates, with Northern/Central Europe as the release validation focus. Do not market unevaluated global accuracy. Requests use server time; a production client cannot choose an evaluation time. Internal replay tools can.

The response contains `now`, `upcoming`, and three `planning` entries. Planning entries use `windows` (an ordered disjoint array) and `darknessStatus` (`windows_available`, `no_darkness`, or `unavailable`). An empty array alone must not be interpreted as midnight sun. `now` answers present conditions. `upcoming` describes the current or next qualifying darkness interval and its evaluated segments, possibly with unknown activity beyond short-term coverage. `planning` shows the next three relevant viewing nights using local noon-to-noon grouping. After midnight, retain the current night if qualifying darkness remains before local noon; otherwise start with tonight. At or after noon, begin with the current period. Include explicit no-window entries during seasons without qualifying darkness. `upcomingStatus` distinguishes a window, no qualifying darkness in 72 hours, and unavailable computation.

All instants are RFC3339 UTC with Z. All intervals are half-open [start, end), strictly increasing, and the timezone is returned. Local date labels must use the returned timezone. Percent values represent cloud cover only; no percentage field describes the chance of seeing aurora.

`endIsSearchLimited` means the interval ends at a search or response boundary rather than a computed transition out of qualifying darkness. It also applies to continuous-darkness windows clipped to a planning period. Segment measurements describe a representative sample; period-wide summaries may return null instead of implying constant weather or solar altitude.

An assessment carries a qualitative rating, action, auroral basis, evidence confidence, explanation, reasons, solar altitude, cloud fraction, and source references. Unknown measurements are null. Required `factors` distinguish darkness classification and weather/local activity states, including missing, stale, unavailable, uncovered, and partial evidence. See [assessment rules](assessment-rules.md). Source objects distinguish `fresh`, `stale`, `unavailable`, and `not_applicable`; provider issue time and fetch time are different. `validFrom` and `validUntil` describe provider coverage, not our cache lifetime. Exact forecast instants are represented by equal coverage timestamps; evaluation tolerances belong to the versioned model.

## UI behavior

- Use stable codes for icons, grouping, and localization; English summary text is an initial convenience. Presentation and final product copy belong to the app ([App copy](copy.md)).
- Render unknown conditions and limitations explicitly. Never turn null into zero or an empty list into a clear-sky claim.
- Label current weather as modeled weather unless observation provenance is present. An auroral model driven by observations is still a model forecast.
- Keep now and upcoming guidance distinct at noon. An attractive planning outlook must not look like a fresh short-term recommendation.
- Display data freshness and attribution. A recently generated response can contain old source data.
- On unsupported enum values, use a neutral unknown fallback. Ignore additive fields. Treat missing required fields as a decoding failure, not favorable conditions.
- Cached offline data must retain its original timestamps and be identified as offline. Never continue a `try_now` recommendation beyond `refreshAfter` without revalidation.

## Errors and caching

400: invalid coordinates or timezone. 429: rate limit with Retry-After. 503: no useful evaluation or evaluator unavailable, with Retry-After. Partial responses use 200 with explicit source states. Errors use the shared Error schema and a request ID; do not leak provider internals. Return private caching instructions; never share personalized location responses in a public cache. ETag support is optional and must consider time-based darkness changes and source ageing, not just unchanged upstream bytes.

`refreshAfter` is the next client revalidation time, not a provider forecast end, promised update, or background-alert tick. The 15-minute alert target (30-minute fallback) does not change this field's meaning or authorize retaining favorable cached guidance between checks. `generatedAt` is the backend evaluation timestamp. The server should align cache expiry with the earliest relevant source expiry or guidance boundary.

## Contract evolution

Freeze semantics and types before implementing independent client integration. Additive optional fields are compatible; removing fields, changing meanings, or changing required structure requires coordination and a new major path when v1 is released. Version the evaluator separately from the API.

## Local development and client integration

Start the service with `cd backend && uv run --offline --frozen aurora-backend serve`. The base URL is `http://127.0.0.1:8000`; the query example above uses a public city centre. For a simulator use that URL; physical-device networking requires explicit binding opt-in and a reachable development host. The app's hosted endpoint is `https://api.zorja.app`; no ATS exception is needed.

Default mode computes real astronomy with unavailable provider factors. It returns useful 200 guidance without promoting synthetic fixtures into fresh data. To enable actual providers locally, set `AURORA_PROVIDER_NETWORK=true` before starting the service. This opens no paid account, uses the confirmed MET contact, and respects persisted upstream expiry/backoff. The local cache has one process owner and at most 64 weather keys; restart does not clear them. Do not enable multiple workers against it.

`/dev/scenarios` and `/dev/replay/{scenario}` remain exact synthetic UI mocks with original timestamps. The development-only command `uv run --offline --frozen aurora-backend forecast-replay stockholm` normalizes recorded provider inputs and exercises `/v1/forecast` in-process. Cities: Stockholm, Oslo, Helsinki, Berlin. Its wrapper identifies recorded replay, preserves capture times, and contains a contract-valid `forecast`. [Current-model recorded outputs](../backend/tests/fixtures/replays/option-a-replay.json) are historical captures evaluated offline, not current guidance. No HTTP evaluation-time parameter exists; extra or repeated query parameters return 400.

Decode all required fields. Preserve nulls, unknown factor states and source timestamps. Current OVATION uses retained-bracket-min-v1: a pair of received target times at most 20 minutes apart supports the minimum overhead signal between them. A single future prediction cannot describe now; it is exposed separately as an exact upcomingActivityInstant. Kp context has no validated geographic mapping; local planning activity stays unknown. Period assessments conservatively summarize uncertainty; inspect windows and their segments for supported modeled weather and darkness. Daylight/cloud blockers can still produce unfavorable guidance when activity is unknown. Empty computed windows mean no darkness only with the matching status. Attribution remains required in the app.

**Immediate expiry:** `refreshAfter` can equal `generatedAt`, expiring the actionable recommendation immediately. This does not require immediate repeated polling; use the independent 60-second request floor described below. Earlier mock checks required strictly later times; clients must not require that inequality or cache an exact-instant `try_now`. `now.interval.start` is the server evaluation instant; its end is the revalidation boundary, with a one-microsecond minimum envelope for schema validity. This envelope is not a claim of continuous activity coverage. Current factors describe the start instant. No released field meaning is silently changed.

All responses use `Cache-Control: private, no-store`, including errors. No response cache or ETag is implemented. Revalidation is controlled by the earliest relevant model-age, cache-expiry, solar, weather or evaluator boundary; five minutes is only the upper fallback. 200 can be fully provider-degraded with useful astronomy; 503 means evaluation could not produce a useful response or exceeded its deadline. 400 and 429 follow the Error schema; 429/503 have `Retry-After: 60`. Every actual forecast/error carries `X-Request-ID`; error bodies carry the matching `requestId`. Synthetic replay errors preserve their authored IDs.

The local process admits 60 valid forecast requests per monotonic minute and at most four active evaluations globally; it does not store per-device/IP/coordinate rate-limit labels. The default response budget is three seconds after admission, reserving 0.2 seconds for CPU evaluation. Completed provider products survive a deadline; unfinished refreshes are cancelled and retained cached evidence is checked independently. CPU overruns return 503 and retain an admission slot until the calculation finishes. Limits are local safeguards, not a production distributed abuse-control design.

`/health/live` means the process is alive. `/health/ready` means the pipeline is configured and its contract loaded; startup failures prevent serving. `/health/providers` is a coordinate-free summary of the last successful forecast, not a polling freshness guarantee; inspect the response's sources for the actual location/time. `/metrics` contains aggregate counters only. The CLI disables access logs, so URLs and precise query coordinates are not emitted. Do not enable proxy/request logging that records query strings when adding deployment infrastructure.

`/openapi.json` serves the frozen v1 repository contract verbatim and `/docs` uses it. There is one schema authority rather than a second automatically generated forecast schema. Full OpenAPI validation and JSON Schema checks run against actual HTTP responses, including errors, alongside scientific/temporal invariants. Auxiliary health/dev routes are operational tools outside the frontend forecast contract.

The v1 interface was frozen after a client review of the 0.2.0 proposal (2026-10-13). Scientific validation of continuous activity coverage remains open.

## Additions from the 0.2.0 review

Additive optional fields preserve decoding by existing clients; the current implementation always emits them. Clients reading older stored responses apply the documented neutral/default fallbacks. No required field was removed or enum changed.

- `minimumRevalidationIntervalSeconds: 60` on Forecast separates request scheduling from scientific expiry. Permit at most one foreground forecast request start per place every 60 seconds, including immediate-expiry responses, failures and retries. Across places, keep at least 5 seconds between any two request starts, so a newly picked place loads at once; a request cancelled by a place change does not start that place's 60 seconds. Honor longer `Retry-After` for every place. (Until 2026-11-13 the 60-second floor applied across location changes; this changes client scheduling only, no field meaning.) For a successful response schedule no earlier than `max(lastRequestStartedAt + 60s, refreshAfter)`. Do not use `generatedAt` as the last request start, and do not loop if a historical response has already expired. Older responses without the new field use 60 seconds. The server's own caps are separate (hosted: 60 forecasts a minute across all instances, plus a per-IP firewall limit, and 60 a minute and 4 at a time per instance; see [hosting](hosting.md)); this policy does not guarantee capacity for unlimited devices.
- After `refreshAfter`, keep the original payload only as historical data labelled with `generatedAt`. Withdraw active `try_now` and present freshness/revalidation status while waiting for the next allowed request. The wire rating/action remains an assessment **as of** the old timestamp; do not present it as a current recommendation. A request floor, a foreground state, or failed refresh never extends source validity. This is the safe alternative to continuing an expired actionable recommendation, which clients must not do.
- `Source.role` has stable values `aurora_short_term`, `aurora_planning`, `weather`, `darkness`. It is independent of free-form `product`, content-hash `id`, and astronomy library version. Unknown/absent roles use neutral source presentation; do not infer future roles from provider names.
- Optional `Reason.sourceId` links source-specific failures to any item in `sources`, including unavailable Kp. This reference may be absent from `assessment.sourceIds`, which lists factor evidence rather than every failing auxiliary feed. If absent or unknown, show generic reasoning. Reasons that describe unsupported local planning activity without a specific feed need no source link. Reason messages use spaces instead of raw enum underscores.
- Optional `planning[].globalKpContext` contains `status`, `sourceId`, and `bins: [{interval, kp}]`. The values are the **global Kp forecast**, on its native 0–9 scale, never a visibility probability or supported local activity. Preserve original UTC three-hour bin boundaries. Include bins intersecting the remaining noon-to-noon period `[max(generatedAt, period.start), period.end)`, allowing a boundary bin to extend beyond the period. Clip only the display to the night, never invent a new sample/resolution. Status `available` requires full remaining-period coverage, `partial` means some bins with gaps, `not_covered` means none, `stale` denotes historical source evidence, and `unavailable` has an empty array. An absent context is unknown context, not Kp zero. Source issue age and attribution are obtained via `sourceId` and stable role. Kp cannot raise a local rating or trigger an alert.

A future segment with fresh Kp coverage has `basis: planning`, even though `factors.activity` remains unknown/not-covered. `basis` names the auroral input context, not confidence in local viewing. Without fresh time-relevant Kp or supported local short-term evidence its basis is unavailable. Current conditions do not get planning basis merely from a global Kp forecast. The synthetic bracket cases have supported short-term activity; the single recorded capture still cannot bracket now.

Clients may aggregate supplied segment weather enums and cloud values for display, preserving gaps and sample resolution. Apply no client scientific thresholds or visibility rules. Three-decimal coordinates are within the weather tolerance; the app sends two decimals (see [privacy](privacy.md)). Keep the selected coordinate stable while the place is unchanged. Coarser rounding is not a cache-capacity solution; 64 distinct coordinates can still exhaust the local cache, which then returns unavailable weather rather than evicting or inventing evidence. Place names stay on the client.


## Earlier coverage gate (historical)

The app's request scheduler and `Forecast.isCurrent` keep polling separate from expiry.

Under the earlier exact-instant model, `try_now` responses were already expired on arrival. A usable current recommendation requires a defensible nonzero interval during which essential evidence supports the assessment. This cannot be obtained by extending a client timer or retaining an old optimistic rating. If activity-evidence metadata is added, the backend must still compute `refreshAfter` as the earliest relevant activity, weather, darkness or freshness boundary. Clients withdraw the recommendation at that boundary; an activity interval alone never overrides an earlier weather/source expiry. The stable evidence-field design below settles the interface representation. The retained-bracket model now provides such an interval experimentally, without scientific calibration.


## Activity-evidence design and v1 migration (original freeze)

`Assessment.activityEvidence` is an additive optional object, emitted by the final service. It contains opaque `temporalPolicy` and `spatialPolicy` identifiers, `forecastInstants`, supporting `sourceIds`, and `supportedInterval` (a strictly increasing UTC half-open Interval or null). The original exact-forecast-instant-v1 model returned null supportedInterval everywhere. The current retained-bracket model populates it for qualified retained brackets using bilinear-overhead-grid-v1, as documented below. Named forecast instants are source metadata, not a duration or proof that a future target describes now. Missing metadata also means unknown continuous support, never an implicit interval.

A future versioned model can populate the interval only after establishing a supported coverage rule. For current guidance that interval must contain generatedAt; refreshAfter must be no later than its end or any earlier essential source/condition boundary. The client continues to withdraw active guidance at refreshAfter. Neither metadata nor a 60-second polling floor can extend it. Source.validFrom/validUntil keep provider semantics; now.interval keeps its response-envelope meaning. Qualitative supported-at-an-instant and interval support are distinct.

The final path is unchanged, `/v1/forecast`; response apiVersion and OpenAPI info.version are now **1.0.0**, an explicit change from the unfrozen 0.2.0 proposal. Clients should accept the frozen v1 identifier. No required shape or enum was removed; activityEvidence can be ignored by the reviewed client, and old cached 0.2.0 responses can remain labelled history with their original timestamps. The immutable initial freeze snapshot is [api/frozen/v1-openapi.json](../api/frozen/v1-openapi.json). Optional additions remain compatible; breaking field/meaning changes require coordination and a new major path. Scientific calibration remains open.

## Hosted transport policy — 2026-11-02

Hosted forecasts are POST-only. The hosted composition returns a fixed 405/Allow: POST for GET, including trailing-slash requests, before validation or provider/cache work; since 2026-11-18 it serves no runtime OpenAPI or `/docs`. Local GET and the full repository/frozen contracts remain unchanged. Clients use POST, without GET fallback. This does not establish privacy for request bodies, responses or URLs already sent to the platform.

Hosted weather entries expire within six hours; the local SQLite cache still keeps permanent reservations. Coordinate precision and all response/scientific semantics are unchanged. Fixtures remain unchanged; verification alone tolerates 1e-10 degrees of absolute libm variation in solarAltitudeDegrees.
